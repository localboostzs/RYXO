Caffeine-free energy drinks: what they are and why they exist

Published: October 26, 2026

A caffeine-free energy drink looks, tastes and is enjoyed like an energy drink — but it contains no caffeine. It is a young and still small category, and it raises a fair question: if caffeine is what makes an energy drink “energise”, what is left? This guide explains what these drinks are, why people choose them and what to look for on the label.

What is in a classic energy drink?

Most classic energy drinks combine a few building blocks: caffeine as the stimulant, sugar or sweeteners, carbonation, flavouring and often taurine and B vitamins. A typical 250 ml can contains roughly 80 mg of caffeine; larger cans can contain well over 100 mg. In the European Union, drinks with more than 150 mg of caffeine per litre have to carry the notice “High caffeine content” together with a warning that they are not recommended for children or for pregnant or breast-feeding women.

What does “caffeine-free” mean?

A caffeine-free energy drink is made without caffeine: no added caffeine and no caffeine-containing ingredients such as coffee or guarana extract. That is different from “decaffeinated”, where caffeine has been removed from an ingredient but traces can remain. For RYXO, the target is 0 mg caffeine per can.

Why do people choose them?

  • Sleep. The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) states that even 100 mg of caffeine may affect sleep duration and sleep patterns in some adults, particularly when consumed close to bedtime. Many people want a cold, fizzy drink in the evening without that trade-off. Our guide Caffeine and sleep has the details.
  • Sensitivity. Some people react strongly to caffeine, for example with restlessness or a racing heart, and prefer to avoid it.
  • Pregnancy and breast-feeding. EFSA considers a caffeine intake of up to 200 mg per day from all sources to be of no safety concern for pregnant and breast-feeding women. Some choose to avoid it more strictly. Health questions belong with a doctor or midwife.
  • Taste and ritual. People love the flavour, the can and the ritual of an energy drink — and do not always want the stimulant that comes with it.

What a caffeine-free energy drink cannot do

Being honest matters here: without caffeine there is no caffeine-driven stimulation. The “energy” in a caffeine-free energy drink is the taste, the colours, the cold fizz and the moment — not a stimulant effect. It does not replace sleep, food or rest.

How to read the label

  • Look for the caffeine content in mg per 100 ml or per can, or for a clear “caffeine-free” statement.
  • Check the ingredient list for coffee, tea, guarana, yerba mate or “natural caffeine”. They all contain caffeine.
  • Look at sugar, sweeteners and other ingredients as you would for any soft drink.
  • Be careful with marketing words such as “energy” or “boost”: they do not tell you whether caffeine is inside.
